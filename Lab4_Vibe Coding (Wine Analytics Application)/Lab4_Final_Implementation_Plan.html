Wine Selection Assistant

From market exploration to a confident, evidence-based bottle choice

Purpose. Build a three-page Plotly Dash application that helps a wine beginner answer one practical question: “Which reviewed wine fits my budget, preferred style, and quality expectations?” The application will turn a large review dataset into a guided decision process rather than a collection of disconnected charts.

Intended users

  • Wine beginners choosing a bottle for personal use or a casual occasion.
  • Users who may know their budget and broad style preference, but not grape varieties, regions, or review terminology.
  • Users who want to inspect the evidence behind a suggestion instead of receiving an opaque recommendation.

Decision-support principles

  • Each page answers one focused question and ends in a clear next action.
  • Every chart supports comparison, filtering, or explanation; redundant charts are excluded.
  • Ratings, prices, sample sizes, and review text remain visible so users can judge trade-offs themselves.
  • Historical reviews support consideration; the app does not claim to predict personal taste or current retail availability.

Overall user workflow

1 · Explore the marketDiscover origins and styles, then select a country or category of interest.
→
2 · Build a shortlistSet budget and quality constraints, compare matching wines, and pin up to three candidates.
→
3 · Read and decideCompare candidates, inspect critic reviews and flavor language, then choose a wine to consider buying.

Application structure and navigation

PageFocused taskOutput carried forward
Explore“Where should I start looking?”Selected country and wine category
Shortlist“Which wines fit my constraints?”Active filters, pinned candidates, selected wine
Review & Decide“Why might this wine suit me?”A transparent final choice and supporting evidence

A persistent top navigation bar will show all three stages and the user’s current stage. Prominent “Use these preferences”, “Review selected wines”, and “Back to shortlist” actions will make the intended path obvious without forcing a linear journey. Selections will survive navigation through shared Dash state.

Planned scope

The first version will reuse the two Lab 2 datasets and the existing wine-themed visual language. It will include linked filtering, three focused dashboards, a recommendation table, and review-text analysis. Live prices, retailer inventory, user accounts, and machine-learning recommendations are intentionally out of scope.

Review Insights & Final Choice

Purpose: explain the shortlisted wines through their review evidence and flavor language, enabling an informed final choice.

ComponentDesignDecision value and interaction
Candidate comparison
Compact table		 Compare the pinned wines side by side: origin, category, variety, average points, median price, review count, and top flavor descriptors. Summarizes trade-offs without requiring users to remember the previous page. Selecting a column or row updates the detail panel.
Wine detail card Show title, winery, origin, variety/category, valid vintage, price, critic points, and the number of underlying reviews. Provides a clear identity for the selected wine and distinguishes missing values from zero.
Flavor profile
Word cloud + descriptor chips		 Generate from review descriptions after text cleaning. Size reflects term frequency; the top eight descriptors also appear as labeled chips. Translates long reviews into approachable flavor cues. A source toggle switches between this wine and same variety, always showing the number of reviews used.
Review evidence
Expandable text panel		 Display the selected wine’s complete critic descriptions, with frequent flavor terms highlighted and taster names where available. Allows users to verify the word cloud and read nuance such as acidity, sweetness, body, and finish.

Final action: “Choose this wine” creates a concise decision summary containing the wine’s key attributes, the user’s active budget/preference criteria, and links back to the evidence. It does not simulate a purchase.

Text-analysis plan

  • Lowercase text, normalize punctuation, and extract meaningful unigrams and bigrams so phrases such as “green apple” are not split apart.
  • Remove standard stop words and non-informative wine vocabulary such as “wine,” “flavor,” “aroma,” and “notes.” Retain domain descriptors including fruits, oak, acidity, sweetness, body, and finish.
  • Display at most 40 terms and require a minimum document frequency to reduce one-off words. Frequencies will be recomputed for the selected source.
  • If the selected wine has too little text, show an explicit sparse-data message and invite the user to switch to same variety; do not silently substitute other reviews.

Feedback and recovery

Use synchronized controls, visible active-filter chips, a result count, and clear selected/pinned styling. Back navigation preserves state. Empty selections return to sensible defaults, while genuinely empty results show recovery guidance.

Accessibility and readability

Pair color with labels, use a consistent color per wine category, provide text equivalents in hover labels and tables, keep controls keyboard accessible, and stack cards on narrow screens.

Data Preparation

Use data/winemag-data-with-year.csv for review records and data/wine_category.csv for the variety-to-category mapping. Load and cache the merged dataset once at startup, then filter it in memory.

Preparation stepPlanned rule
Join and standardizeLeft-join categories on variety; trim text, standardize category labels, and map unmapped varieties to other. Convert US to United States for the map.
Numeric fieldsConvert points, price, and year to numeric values. Keep points and price in their observed ranges (80–100 and $4–$3,300); use a logarithmic display for the long-tailed price distribution.
Missing and invalid valuesLabel missing text as Unknown. Keep missing prices in unfiltered exploration, mark them as unavailable, and exclude them from price-based plots or budget-confirmed results with a visible count. Treat implausible vintages outside 1980–2026 as missing.
Wine-level aggregationGroup reviews by title and winery for candidate comparison. Retain review IDs for traceability; aggregate average points, median price, valid vintage, review count, and combined descriptions.

Technical Implementation

Stack and setup

  • Create an isolated environment with python -m venv .venv.
  • Use Python, pandas, NumPy, Plotly, and Dash; add wordcloud for flavor rendering.
  • Use Dash Pages for the three routes and dcc.Store for shared filter/selection state.
  • Reuse Lab 2’s cached loader, figure helpers, empty-state pattern, and burgundy/cream styling.

Proposed structure

  • app.py — application shell and navigation
  • pages/explore.py, shortlist.py, reviews.py
  • src/data_loader.py — loading, cleaning, aggregation
  • src/text_analysis.py — descriptor extraction
  • components/ — shared filters, cards, and tables
  • assets/styles.css — responsive visual system

Implementation Sequence

  1. Foundation: prepare the environment, validate the join, implement cleaning and wine-level aggregation, and confirm summary values against the raw data.
  2. Decision flow: build the navigation shell and shared state, then create Explore and carry country/category selections into Shortlist.
  3. Candidate selection: implement filters, linked scatter/table outputs, sorting, pinning, and empty-state recovery.
  4. Review explanation: add candidate comparison, detail/review panels, descriptor extraction, and the final choice summary.
  5. Iterative refinement: test the complete journey, remove elements that do not change a decision, clarify labels, and improve responsive behavior.

Validation and Completion Criteria

  • A new user can move from market exploration to a final wine without instructions outside the app.
  • Map, dropdown, scatter, and table selections remain synchronized across pages.
  • Every chart updates correctly under combined filters and communicates its sample size.
  • Missing prices, invalid vintages, sparse reviews, and zero-result filters produce explicit, non-breaking states.
  • Pinning is limited to three wines; the selected wine and its review evidence always match.
  • Word-cloud terms come only from the labeled source and can be verified in the displayed reviews.
  • Prices and ratings in aggregated views match reproducible pandas calculations.
  • The layout remains usable at desktop and narrow-screen widths, with no chart lacking a clear decision purpose.

End-to-end acceptance scenario. A beginner selects France on Explore, chooses white wine, sets a $20–$50 budget and a minimum of 88 points on Shortlist, pins two high-rated candidates, compares their review language on Review & Decide, and leaves with one wine whose price, rating, and flavor evidence match the stated preferences.
